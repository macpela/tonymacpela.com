Tony Macpela · Bucharest

House Crash

Interactive installation · Oversized Physical Controls

House Crash is the shortest card in the installation list, and the page refuses to pad it into a ruleset. The published lines are: two big buttons, a collapsing cityscape, architecture as enemy. The tech line is oversized physical controls. That is a complete aesthetic position even when it is not a complete manual. The body is not tracked. The hand is not read as a sign. You hit something large. The city in the picture is already failing, and the architecture is cast as the adversary rather than as a surface to be mapped.

An older note in an earlier fallback described dodging buildings left and right. The live sheet no longer says that, so this page does not restore it. What the three sentences still support is a physical, almost comic aggression toward the interface: buttons big enough to be scenery, a picture of collapse, a villain that is the built environment. Next to Noroc sa ai, the other game-like work, House Crash is the blunt one. Noroc wants to feel like a real cabinet, with a topper and themed sound. House Crash wants the control to be obviously too big. One mimics a machine. The other makes the machine impossible to ignore. Both are interactive. They do not share a build.
